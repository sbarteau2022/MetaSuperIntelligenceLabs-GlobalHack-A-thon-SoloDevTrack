First-stage diagnostic: MLX's `embed_tokens` output and post-normalization embedding both have `mlx.core.bfloat16` dtype; Rust's `embed_token` returns f32. On the BOS token's 6656-wide post-normalization embedding, 219 values matched exactly as f32, max absolute difference was `0.0433197021484375`, and mean absolute difference was `0.00011940314358910725`. Rounding the Rust embedding to bfloat16 in an offline comparison increased exact matches to 6339 and reduced mean absolute difference to `0.00004606980543870192`; the remaining max difference was `0.015625`. This locates a numerical divergence before attention, but does not prove all downstream differences are caused by precision alone. A Rust precision-profile ablation should be separate from performance tuning.

Second-stage diagnosis of the raw embedding (before scaleless RMS norm): the checkpoint's BOS row out_scale is 15.987359046936035. The MLX ScaledQuantizedEmbedding computes a bfloat16 quantized row, multiplies by the f32 row scale, then casts back to bfloat16. Rust's dequantize_row_zero_copy computes (q * scale + bias) * out_scale in f32 without either rounding. Comparing the 6656 output components: raw Rust versus MLX had 219 exact matches; rounding the final Rust result once gave 6306; replaying the two MLX roundings as bf16(bf16(rust_raw / out_scale) * out_scale) gave **6656 exact matches, max absolute difference 0**. The equality is measured on this real checkpoint row, not a proof for every row or projection. It isolates the first divergence to the embedding precision contract. The next Rust implementation should encode this as a named checkpoint numerical profile and verify other real rows before promotion.

The Rust embedding-only ablation applies those two roundings directly to the packed row decode and rounds the post-embedding RMS result. Its BOS post-normalization embedding matched MLX **6656/6656** exactly. The full final logits did not improve: max absolute difference changed from 0.13231056928634644 to 0.13553541898727417 and mean absolute difference from 0.019950137394190617 to 0.020016538511877454; argmax stayed 13796. At layer 0, the pre-attention norm matched MLX at only 6016/6656 components after bfloat16 rounding of Rust's output; the fused Q projection matched at 6525/8192 components after the same final rounding. A full MLX numerical profile must include norm and quantized projection boundaries, not only embedding.
